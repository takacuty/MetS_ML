
Introduction

Metabolic syndrome (MetS) is a major risk factor for 
cardiovascular disease (CVD) and type 2 diabetes mellitus. As populations age worldwide, extending healthy life expectancy has become an urgent priority. Japan, a super-aged society, introduced the national “Specific Health Checkups (SHC) and Specific Health Guidance” program in 2008 to detect and address MetS early. However, MetS prevalence continues to rise more than a decade after the program began. The current system may contribute to this trend by classifying individuals as high risk only when they have abdominal obesity. Yet multiple metabolic abnormalities can confer elevated cardiovascular risk among individuals who do not meet this criterion. Predictive models based on health checkup data are therefore needed to forecast disease onset accurately and enable preemptive interventions for high-risk individuals. 

Predictive models for MetS onset are being actively developed worldwide. Risk scores based on SHC data have also been reported in Japan. Recent studies have applied machine learning to disease risk prediction. Despite these advances, existing studies of MetS have largely focused on diagnosing current MetS or predicting its onset within one to two years. Because MetS develops after metabolic abnormalities accumulate over many years, early assessment of future onset risk is important. Incident MetS cases are often underrepresented in health checkup datasets, creating class imbalance and hindering accurate detection. Resampling and threshold adjustment have been proposed to address this imbalance, but their effectiveness for long-term MetS prediction remains unclear. The primary challenge is therefore to identify an optimal modeling method that accurately predicts MetS onset.

Earlier MetS prediction models often limited the number of predictors to reduce overfitting and improve interpretability. Studies by the authors have shown that MetS risk is influenced by diverse factors, including standard blood markers and lifestyle factors. Moreover, multiple risk factors can interact synergistically to increase MetS risk. Excessive variable restriction may therefore overlook important predictors and interaction effects, reducing predictive accuracy. Recent predictive modeling studies recommend leveraging all available data and evaluating internal validity during model development. A second challenge is therefore to improve predictive accuracy while maintaining clinically acceptable interpretability.

Against this background, this study aims to develop a model that predicts MetS onset within five years using a large-scale health checkup dataset and to evaluate its predictive performance and interpretability. The study addresses three key aspects. First, it targets medium- to long-term prediction with high clinical relevance, enabling preventive interventions before disease onset—an opportunity unavailable with short-term models. Second, it compares learning strategies for imbalanced data to identify the strategy that yields the most reliable screening performance. Third, it uses all available health checkup variables without prior selection and applies SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) to interpret model predictions. This approach captures nonlinear relationships and interactions among risk factors while enabling interpretation of individual risk profiles. Beyond improving predictive accuracy, the framework offers a novel approach to integrating large-scale health checkup data into clinical practice and public health policy.

Methods

Data Source

This study used 12 years of SHC data (fiscal years 2011–2022) obtained from the Kokuho Database (KDB), which is managed by the Gunma Association of National Health Insurance Organizations. The KDB is a public administrative database that integrates local residents’ health checkup records and medical claims data, covering approximately 40% of insured residents in Gunma Prefecture.

Study Population

Figure 1 shows the participant selection process. The initial population comprised 1,069,543 National Health Insurance beneficiary records from Gunma Prefecture for individuals who underwent SHC during fiscal years 2011–2017 (2011: 150,610; 2012: 154,579; 2013: 156,454; 2014: 156,531; 2015: 155,690; 2016: 151,068; 2017: 144,611). Incident MetS was assessed using health checkup data collected five years after baseline. The following exclusion criteria were applied: 594,166 records without five-year follow-up data; 326,513 duplicate records, with only the most recent visit retained for participants with multiple records; and 294 records with missing data that precluded MetS assessment. In addition, 11,259 individuals with a history of stroke, heart disease, or chronic renal failure recorded in the standard questionnaire and 18,299 individuals who met the MetS criteria at baseline were excluded. These criteria yielded a final analytical population of 119,012 participants. Baseline characteristics were also compared between the analytical cohort and the excluded group to assess potential selection bias.
